# Quoridor AlphaZero Epoch 700 Training Collapse and QuoridorNet v2 Remediation

## 1. Executive Summary

By Epoch 700, the original Quoridor AlphaZero training pipeline reached a state of representational and tactical stagnation. Although catastrophic failure modes from early development (such as zero-gradient collapse and unmasked cross-entropy errors) had been resolved, the training process plateaued into a severe local minimum. 

This document details:
1. The diagnostic root causes of the Epoch 700 failure.
2. The reasons why existing checkpoint weights and replay buffer data cannot be reused.
3. The complete technical log of all architectural, search, and self-play remediations implemented across Phases 1, 2, and 3, along with their respective refinements.

---

## 2. Root Cause Analysis of the Epoch 700 Failure

### A. Closed-Loop Self-Play Echo Chamber
All 700 epochs were generated strictly via self-play between identical copies of the active model. Without exposure to external opponents or tactical benchmarks, the self-play dynamic converged into an artificial equilibrium:
- The agent developed an over-reliance on repetitive wall placement patterns.
- Because both agents shared the same blind spots, neither player punished naive pawn rushing or unorthodox wall traps.
- When tested against heuristic or minimax baselines, the model struggled to respond to straightforward forward pawn marches.

### B. Shallow MCTS Exploration and the Wall Placement Trap
Quoridor has an asymmetric action space consisting of up to 12 pawn movements and 128 wall placements (140 total actions).
- Under the fixed 400-simulation budget of v1, visit density was less than 3 visits per legal action.
- Wall placements provide immediate, short-term path distance increases to the opponent. Under shallow search depths, MCTS preferentially selected walls because the tree could not look deep enough to realize that exhausting wall inventory early handicaps the late game.
- The policy prior distribution concentrated heavily on wall placements (over 85% of total prior mass), leaving pawn moves underexplored.

### C. Endgame Move Shuffling and Absence of Deterministic Solvers
In Quoridor, once both players exhaust their walls (or when path margins are settled), the game transitions into a pure pawn race.
- In v1, the model was forced to evaluate endgame states using neural network inference and stochastic MCTS.
- Noisy leaf evaluations caused pawns to oscillate back and forth rather than marching directly to their baseline, triggering 3-fold repetition penalties or maximum move limits (300 moves), yielding high draw rates and muddying value targets with `-0.2` penalties.

### D. Architectural Bottlenecks in the v1 Neural Network
- **Scalar Mass Overload**: Early iterations tiled scalar metadata (remaining walls, path lengths) as uniform 9x9 channels across the CNN trunk, dominating sparse spatial pawn features.
- **Dense Linear Readout Layer**: The v1 policy head used a dense `Linear(324, 140)` mapping from flattened convolutional features. This destroyed the 2D spatial relationship between board coordinates and wall placement indices, resulting in slow and noisy gradient propagation.
- **Absence of Intermediate Spatial Targets**: The network received no spatial gradient feedback regarding path bottlenecks or multi-path critical corridors.

### E. Learning Rate Schedule Stagnation
The v1 training loop used a step decay schedule (`StepLR(step_size=50, gamma=0.8)`). By Epoch 700, the learning rate had decayed to `8.25e-6`. At this magnitude, the optimizer could no longer update convolutional kernels significantly or escape the established policy bias.

### F. Replay Buffer Pollution
The `C:\quoridor_data` directory contained approximately 700 epochs of self-play trajectories. Because recent epochs were generated by a policy with high draw rates and excessive move counts (averaging over 150 moves per game), sampling from this buffer continuously reinforced the degenerate playstyle.

## 3. Comprehensive Log of Upgrades and Remediations

To resolve these failure modes, the entire system was overhauled across three phases and a subsequent refinement pass.

### Phase 1: Search and Self-Play Efficiency

1. **3-Tier Wall Legality Filter**
   - **Files**: `cpp/quoridor_simulator.h`, `cpp/quoridor_simulator.cpp`, `js/engine.js`
   - **Mechanism**:
     - *Tier 1 (Bitboard Geometric Check)*: Validates boundary constraints and wall overlaps in $O(1)$ using 64-bit integer bitmasks.
     - *Tier 2 (Slatton's 2-Contact Rule)*: A newly placed wall cannot disconnect a player unless it connects to at least two borders or existing wall segments. Walls with fewer than two contact points bypass BFS shortest-path validation entirely.
     - *Tier 3 (Cached Path Intersection Check)*: If a candidate wall does not intersect the cached shortest path of either player, it cannot lengthen or block that path, bypassing expensive full re-computations.

2. **KataGo First Play Urgency (FPU) Reduction**
   - **Files**: `cpp/mcts.h`, `cpp/mcts.cpp`, `js/ai-mcts.js`
   - **Mechanism**: Unvisited child nodes are assigned a value of $Q_{\text{parent}} - c_{\text{FPU}}$ (with $c_{\text{FPU}} = 0.20$) instead of default 0.0. This prevents unvisited actions from being artificially favored in losing positions or heavily penalized in winning positions.

3. **KataGo Forced Playouts and Policy Target Pruning (PTP)**
   - **Files**: `cpp/mcts.cpp`, `js/ai-mcts.js`
   - **Mechanism**:
     - At the root node, each child is allocated a minimum number of forced playouts: $N_{\text{forced}}(a) = \lfloor k_{\text{forced}} \cdot P(a) \cdot \sqrt{N_{\text{root}}} \rfloor$ with $k_{\text{forced}} = 2.0$.
     - When constructing policy targets for standard PUCT searches, forced playouts are subtracted from non-best children: $N'(a) = \max(0, N(a) - N_{\text{forced}}(a))$. This sharpens the target policy on proven moves and eliminates exploratory noise.

4. **Playout Cap Randomization (PCR)**
   - **Files**: `run_actor.py`, `train_alphazero.py`
   - **Mechanism**: In self-play, 25% of turns run a full deep search (800 simulations) while 75% of turns run a fast-cap search (120 simulations). Only deep search turns are recorded into the replay buffer, speeding up data generation by 3x to 4x while preserving deep policy targets.

5. **Extended Temperature Schedule & Bootstrapped Value Target Mixing**
   - **Files**: `run_actor.py`, `train_alphazero.py`
   - **Mechanism**:
     - Temperature decay was extended from ply 10 to ply 20: $\tau = \max(0.1, 1.0 - \frac{\text{move}}{20.0})$, followed by deterministic $\arg\max$ from move 20 onward.
     - Value targets are bootstrapped by mixing the terminal discounted outcome $z$ with the root MCTS evaluation $q_{\text{root}}$: $v_{\text{target}} = \lambda z + (1 - \lambda) q_{\text{root}}$ with $\lambda = 0.70$.

6. **Cosine Annealing Learning Rate & Gradient Norm Clipping**
   - **Files**: `train_alphazero.py`
   - **Mechanism**: Replaced `StepLR` with `CosineAnnealingLR(T_max=epochs, eta_min=1e-5)` starting from `lr = 0.00015`. Added global gradient norm clipping (`torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_(max_norm=1.0)`).

### Phase 2: State Tensor & Neural Network Architecture Upgrade

1. **State Tensor Upgrade (11, 9, 9)**
   - **Files**: `cpp/mcts.cpp`, `js/ai-mcts.js`, `quoridor_model.py`
   - **Channels 0-3**: One-hot active pawn, opponent pawn, horizontal walls (8x8 zero-padded to 9x9), vertical walls (8x8 zero-padded to 9x9).
   - **Channels 4-5**: 2D Reverse-BFS distance maps for active player and opponent. Each cell $(x, y)$ stores the shortest-path distance from $(x, y)$ to the player's goal row, normalized by 20.0.
   - **Channels 6-7**: All-Shortest-Paths DAG Criticality masks. Computed by combining forward BFS path counts with reverse BFS path counts to identify bottleneck cells that lie on all optimal paths.
   - **Channels 8-9**: Active and opponent wall inventories normalized by 10.0.
   - **Channel 10**: Tempo and jump-parity checkerboard feature.

2. **KataGo Global Pooling Residual Trunk**
   - **Files**: `quoridor_model.py`
   - **Mechanism**: Residual blocks incorporate Squeeze-and-Excitation attention with combined spatial mean and spatial max pooling: $y = [\text{mean\_pool}(x), \text{max\_pool}(x)]$. This allows the network to maintain awareness of localized critical wall gaps while tracking overall board territory.

3. **Decoupled Spatial Wall Convolutional Policy Head**
   - **Files**: `quoridor_model.py`
   - **Mechanism**:
     - *Pawn Sub-Head*: Conv1x1 $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU $\to$ Flatten $\to$ Linear $\to$ 12 logits.
     - *Wall Sub-Head*: Conv1x1 $\to$ BatchNorm $\to$ ReLU $\to$ Conv2x2 (stride 1, no padding) producing a spatial $(2, 8, 8)$ grid. Reshaped into 64 horizontal wall logits and 64 vertical wall logits.
     - Preserves 2D spatial locality for wall placements.

4. **Multi-Task Auxiliary Readout Heads**
   - **Files**: `quoridor_model.py`, `train_alphazero.py`, `run_actor.py`
   - **Auxiliary Head A (End-of-Game Path Length)**: Predicts normalized terminal path distances $[d_{\text{active}}^{\text{end}}, d_{\text{opp}}^{\text{end}}]$ in $[0, 1]$.
   - **Auxiliary Head B (Final Wall Ownership)**: Emits a $(2, 8, 8)$ grid predicting wall placement density at game conclusion.
   - **Auxiliary Head C (Opponent Next Move)**: Predicts the opponent's response policy (140 logits).
   - **Deployment**: `ONNXDeployWrapper` in `quoridor_model.py` strips auxiliary heads during export to ensure `public/quoridor_model.onnx` has zero runtime inference overhead in the browser.

### Phase 3: Transposition Tables, Gumbel AlphaZero & Solvers

1. **Bitboard DAG Transposition Table**
   - **Files**: `cpp/mcts.h`, `cpp/mcts.cpp`, `cpp/quoridor_simulator.h`, `cpp/quoridor_simulator.cpp`, `js/engine.js`, `js/ai-mcts.js`
   - **Mechanism**:
     - State representation includes 64-bit unsigned integers `h_bitboard` and `v_bitboard`.
     - Canonical state hashing uses SplitMix64 and canonical horizontal reflection: $x \to 7 - x$ for walls and $x \to 8 - x$ for pawns.
     - Caches $(P, V)$ evaluations to eliminate redundant neural network evaluations on transposed paths.

2. **Dynamic $\sigma(\hat{q}(a))$ Gumbel Sequential Halving**
   - **Files**: `cpp/mcts.cpp`, `js/ai-mcts.js`
   - **Mechanism**: Root exploration uses Gumbel Sequential Halving ($m=16$) at low simulation counts ($\le 128$).
   - Completion scores scale dynamically with the maximum visit count across root children:
     $$\sigma(\hat{q}(a)) = (c_{\text{visit}} + \max_b N(b)) \cdot c_{\text{scale}} \cdot \hat{q}(a)$$
     with $c_{\text{visit}} = 50.0$ and $c_{\text{scale}} = 1.0$. As visits concentrate on candidate subsets across halving rounds, empirical $Q$-values progressively override initial prior logits and Gumbel noise.

3. **Gumbel Completed Q Policy Targets & Non-Root Deterministic Selection**
   - **Files**: `cpp/mcts.cpp`, `js/ai-mcts.js`
   - **Mechanism**:
     - At non-root nodes during Gumbel search, selection is deterministic: $\arg\max_a \left(\text{prior}(a) - \frac{N(a)}{1 + \sum_b N(b)}\right)$.
     - Policy targets generated by Gumbel searches use Completed Q-values: $\pi'(a) = \text{softmax}(\ln(p(a)) + \sigma(\text{completedQ}(a)))$. This prevents target distortion caused by the halving schedule.

4. **Zero-Wall Race Solver with Head-to-Head Jump Parity Guard**
   - **Files**: `cpp/quoridor_simulator.cpp`, `js/engine.js`
   - **Mechanism**:
     - Evaluates deterministic endgames when walls are exhausted or single-player wall-out conditions occur.
     - **Jump Parity Guard**: On tight path margins ($|l_1 - l_2| \le 1$), the solver computes both players' forward and reverse BFS distance maps to test if their shortest-path DAGs intersect. If the paths collide head-on, straight jumps can flip the race winner depending on move tempo; in this case, the solver returns `0` (unresolved) to let MCTS explore the jump tactics.
     - When DAGs are disjoint, or when the distance margin is $\ge 2$ full plies, the solver returns $+1.0$ or $-1.0$ in $O(1)$.

5. **Channel 10 Tempo & Jump-Parity Feature**
   - **Files**: `cpp/mcts.cpp`, `js/ai-mcts.js`, `quoridor_model.py`
   - **Mechanism**:
     - Formatted as an alternating checkerboard pattern:
       - Even cells (`(x+y)%2 == 0`): Path advantage $(d_{\text{opp}} - d_{\text{active}}) / 16.0 \in [-1, 1]$.
       - Odd cells (`(x+y)%2 == 1`): Jump-parity initiative indicator ($+1.0$ if Manhattan pawn distance is even, indicating active player jump initiative; $-1.0$ if odd).
     - Invariant under horizontal reflection and 180-degree perspective rotation.

6. **Mixed-Opponent Self-Play Curriculum**
   - **Files**: `run_actor.py`
   - **Mechanism**: 20% of self-play games are allocated to spar against the depth-2 Minimax bot. Minimax moves execute without GPU inference. Only turns played by the active AlphaZero model are recorded into the replay buffer with the true game outcome.

## 4. Pipeline Launcher Reference (`start_training.py`)

A unified launcher script was created to manage the multi-process training environment:

- **Script**: `start_training.py` (and batch wrapper `start_training.bat`)
- **Key Flags**:
  - `--fresh`: Moves the old checkpoint to `checkpoint_backup/`, renames `C:\quoridor_data` to `C:\quoridor_data_v1_backup`, resets `public/training_state.json`, and starts training at Epoch 1.
  - `--actors <n>`: Number of parallel self-play workers (default: 4, recommended: 4 to 8).
  - `--simulations <n>`: Deep search simulations (default: 800).
  - `--fast_simulations <n>`: Fast search simulations for PCR (default: 120).
  - `--windows`: Launches processes in separate native OS command prompt windows.
  - Omission of `--windows`: Runs the pipeline as a managed child process directly inside the current terminal.
